{{ section_title("Architecture", "How a frame becomes a decision") }}
  1. 01

    Offline setup

    Zone polygons and per-zone PPE requirements, a clause map, and the OSHA text indexed for retrieval.

  2. 02

    Perception deterministic · no LLM

    frame → detector → assessment + zone → confirmation over time → a ViolationEvent. Everything up to here is unit-testable; no language model touches a compliance finding.

  3. 03

    Agent layer

    Assessor says what happened and under what rule. Adjudicator picks the proportionate response. Recorder commits and reads back — deliberately not an agent, because committing a row is not a judgement call. A guardrail stops any escalation without a human.

  4. 04

    State & interface

    SQLite, a local evidence store, this service, and these pages.

At helmet recall 0.796, roughly one worn helmet in five is not detected. Single-frame escalation would therefore mislabel a compliant worker about 20% of the time.

That is why a violation must be sustained across a tracked window before it becomes actionable, and why the measured per-class recall ships inside every event — so the agent can weigh an absence appropriately.

{{ section_title("Honesty", "Known limitations") }}
  • Worker identity does not persist. Tracker IDs do not survive a session, a camera hand-off, or a worker leaving frame. "Third time this week" therefore cannot come from tracking alone — a supervisor binds the name, on the Identify page.
  • Domain gap. The detector is trained on a construction-PPE dataset (1,416 images). Other camera angles, lighting and mounting heights will do worse — which is what Monitoring watches for.
  • No dedicated hi-vis or hand-protection clause in construction. Scope is 29 CFR 1926. Vest and glove findings cite site policy supported by 1926.95's hazard-assessment duty, and say so rather than inventing a clause.
  • Small-object localisation is capped by label quality. mAP50-95 sat near 0.435 across four training configurations.
